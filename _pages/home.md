CyTRAIL Lab carries out research in offensive security and digital forensics. We investigate software vulnerabilities and the ways they can be exploited. Based on this knowledge, we analyze the digital evidence produced by attacks and develop new forensic and defensive methods. We also aim to build realistic security and forensic datasets to support the development and evaluation of more effective AI models in these areas.

<div class="callout" markdown="0">
<p>Effective cybersecurity defense requires understanding software vulnerabilities, how and by whom they can be exploited, and how resulting incidents can be investigated.</p>
</div>

## Our research pipeline

<div class="pipeline" markdown="0" aria-label="vulnerability, then threat, then evidence, then defense">
<span class="chip chip-muted">vulnerability</span><span class="pipeline-arrow" aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span>
<span class="chip chip-muted">threat</span><span class="pipeline-arrow" aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span>
<span class="chip chip-muted">evidence</span><span class="pipeline-arrow" aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span>
<span class="chip chip-muted">defense</span>
</div>

Our researchers identify vulnerabilities that make attacks possible and study the threats and exploitation techniques related to them. Identified vulnerabilities are reported through responsible disclosure so that they can be fixed. We then examine the evidence generated by attacks and use these findings to develop improved forensic and defensive methods. In this way, our offensive security research and digital forensics research are closely connected.
